* Status and verdict

This report records the completed filter-agnostic CAGRA study.  The production GPU, exact, and CPU
runs are complete; their strict analyzers pass; the six-page double-blind paper builds; and its PDF
is cryptographically bound to the staged inputs.  The benchmark code remains experimental and
does not add a supported public cuVS API or ABI.

The result is not one universal filtered-search algorithm.  It is a decomposition of three failure
regimes and two GPU mechanisms that isolate them:

1. *Retention loss.*  CAGRA may evaluate a high-quality legal node and later evict it from its fused
   navigation/result buffer.  CAGRA-Retain adds a bounded top-=k= passing-result accumulator without
   changing traversal.  At the highest-recall B0 point on YFCC-10M, recall rises from 0.5976 to
   0.7714 while median QPS changes from 23,218 to 22,066.
2. *Region-entry loss.*  Raw-distance traversal can spend its budget outside a legal region that is
   easy to search after entry.  On ArXiv-EMIS, CAGRA-NaviX's legal-only persistent frontier and
   adaptive one-/two-hop expansion reaches 0.9972 recall at B0, versus 0.6909 for Base and 0.7875
   for Retain.
3. *Predicate-connectivity loss.*  YFCC predicates are extremely sparse and fragmented: median
   selectivity is 0.1395%, and 71.16% of queries are at or below 1%.  Retention and NaviX both remain
   below 0.9 at B0.  Deeper Retain traversal reaches 0.9123 only at 2,783 QPS, showing that high
   recall is reachable but not within the default work budget.

The practical conclusion is that query-time filtering on an unchanged graph can fix retention and
some region-entry failures, but traversal policy alone does not efficiently solve all sparse
predicate-connectivity cases.  The next design should combine bounded retention and transient
bridges with exact routing or a richer, GPU-friendly, predicate-agnostic topology.

* Research scope

The research question is: how far can query-time, predicate-agnostic filtering go on an unchanged
CAGRA graph?  The graph is constructed only from vectors.  At query time, each query supplies a
resident packed bitmap identifying legal source IDs.

Included:

- CAGRA =G=64=, =IG=128= for YFCC and =G=32=, =IG=64= for ArXiv; SINGLE_CTA;
  squared L2; =k=10=; =max_queries=512=.
- YFCC-10M and ArXiv EM, EMIS, and R, with 10,000 queries per throughput point.
- CAGRA-Base, CAGRA-Retain, CAGRA-NaviX, and matched deterministic passing-seed controls.
- B0 plus selected deeper YFCC-Retain points.
- A resident-bitmap masked exact control.
- Native FAISS-NaviX and ACORN CPU curves as qualitative algorithmic context.

Excluded:

- A supported public filter API, FAVOR scoring, MULTI_CTA, MULTI_KERNEL, persistent search, or
  dynamic batching.
- Bitmap construction/upload and UDF execution.  All graph-search methods consume identical
  resident bitmaps.
- A GPU ACORN/MCI index, filter-aware graph construction, distributed execution, and out-of-core
  search.
- Absolute CPU/GPU speedup claims; CPU systems use different graphs, hardware, and timing regions.

* GPU implementation

** CAGRA-Base

Base preserves native CAGRA's fused, raw-distance-ordered =itopk= state.  The bitmap is checked for
output legality, but predicate membership does not affect candidate ordering.  Native fused Base
may repeat a valid ID when output is underfilled; evaluation therefore gives each distinct output
ID at most one Recall@10 credit and reports the duplicate-query rate separately.

** CAGRA-Retain

Retain leaves parent selection, neighbor expansion, visited handling, distance computation, and
termination unchanged.  In parallel it keeps a bounded raw-distance top-=k= accumulator containing
only legal nodes observed anywhere during traversal.  Final neighbors/distances are read from this
accumulator rather than from the fused navigation buffer.

For =k=10=, the accumulator has a bounded 84-byte CTA-local state footprint.  It removes selection
loss without pretending to improve reachability: if traversal never evaluates a legal ground-truth
node, Retain cannot recover it.

** CAGRA-NaviX

NaviX begins from deterministic legal bitmap seeds and maintains a legal-only persistent frontier.
It applies the adaptive-local NaviX policy: use one-hop expansion when local legal yield is high;
otherwise inspect grandchildren through rejected one-hop bridge vertices.  Bridge vertices are
transient and are not admitted to the persistent legal frontier.

The GPU implementation preserves CAGRA's fused =itopk= structure and avoids allocating a
=degree^2= shared-memory buffer.  A fixed =W x degree= bridge buffer is consumed in tiles.  Warps
cooperatively load adjacency, ballot legal grandchildren, run visited checks, compute distances
only for unique admitted nodes, and commit them in deterministic bridge order.  Bitmap row
metadata is hoisted once per CTA; aligned identity-ID rows use a direct packed-word test, while an
exact fallback handles unaligned rows and source-index remapping.

One source implementation is compiled into degree-32 and degree-64 plan fragments.  Degree 32
uses one warp tile per adjacency row; degree 64 uses two coalesced tiles.  The adaptive-local
cutovers are =P<=4= blind, =5..12= directed, and =P>=13= one-hop for G32; for G64 the directed
range is =5..25= and one-hop begins at =P=26=.  Degree is fixed per index and dispatched once when
the search plan is selected.

** Bitmap seed selection and matched controls

A separate GPU seed-selection kernel scans each query's packed bitmap and emits the first =k=
legal internal graph IDs.  The NaviX search kernel initializes only those valid seeds, inserts only
them into the visited table, and does not mix in ordinary random CAGRA starts.  Seed selection is
included in reported search timing.

Matched controls give CAGRA-Base and CAGRA-Retain the same deterministic legal seeds.  They isolate
seed availability from later traversal and retention policy; they are ablations, not primary
methods.

** B0 and timing

=max_iterations=0= selects CAGRA's automatic budget rather than zero work:

#+begin_example
B0 = floor(L / W) + g(N,D)
#+end_example

The size/degree allowance =g= is 5 for both graph families.  Thus =L=512, =W=2 corresponds to 261
iterations on both YFCC and ArXiv.  A point covers 10,000 queries.  ArXiv uses one call; YFCC uses
four 2,048-query shards and one 1,808-query shard.  YFCC QPS is exactly =10000 / sum(five search
times)=, never the mean of shard QPS.

The timed GPU graph call includes NaviX bitmap seed selection when applicable.  Bitmaps are already
resident; predicate materialization and upload are excluded for every graph method.

* Correctness and experiment protocol

- Every throughput point has three complete repetitions of 10,000 queries.  Figures show medians
  with min--max bars.
- A separate 1,000-query correctness gate precedes throughput.
- Output recall uses distinct valid IDs.  Legal-ID, sentinel order/distance, and duplicate checks
  are explicit.
- Final GPU analysis fully hashes resident base, query, GT, bitmap, and index inputs; no hash-skip
  path is accepted for paper staging.
- Exact-search correctness requires native squared-L2 cutoff recall of one, zero cutoff errors,
  zero strict-prefix errors, and correct invalid sentinels.
- CPU analysis requires the complete 5-method x 4-workload x 10-=efSearch= Cartesian product,
  three repetitions, and 10,000 queries.  Approximate underfilling is a search-quality diagnostic;
  filter violations, duplicate IDs, and malformed sentinel outputs are correctness failures.

* Interpretation

** Why the accumulator helps

CAGRA's =itopk= serves two jobs: navigation frontier and result store.  A legal node can be useful
as a final answer yet lose its slot to a raw-distance candidate needed for navigation.  Retain
separates these lifetimes.  The accumulator is necessary when GT-seen exceeds returned recall; it
is not caused by correlation, and it cannot discover an unseen node.

** Why EMIS is hard and NaviX succeeds

EMIS's global selectivity (15.24%) is not especially small.  Its legal items arrive unusually late
in raw-distance rank, so filter-blind traversal wastes work before entering the matching category.
Once entered, the category is strongly connected.  NaviX changes traversal, not merely output
retention, and therefore converts a region-entry problem into a passing-only graph walk.

** Why YFCC remains hard

YFCC combines extremely low per-query selectivity with poor legal-frontier continuity.  In a
separate in-kernel-seeded diagnostic, 96.4% of NaviX queries terminate by frontier exhaustion and
33.3% are underfilled, including 29.9% that had a valid legal seed.  About 24.5% of expanded parents
have no legal first-hop neighbor.  Forcing more two-hop work did not improve recall in the
stratified hard-case oracle.  The issue is therefore not just seed acquisition or an overly
conservative adaptive threshold; one rejected bridge often does not expose a connected legal
subgraph.

* Limitations

- One L4 GPU, YFCC =G=64/=IG=128 and ArXiv =G=32/=IG=64 CAGRA graphs, SINGLE_CTA,
  squared L2, and =k=10=.
- Resident bitmaps omit predicate evaluation, construction, transfer, caching, and invalidation.
- YFCC graph search is native uint8, whereas its exact control converts bytes exactly to float32
  and therefore moves four times as many coordinate bytes.
- B0 plus the selected explicit depths is not proof over every possible budget.
- CPU and GPU methods use different graph families and system boundaries.
- Two dataset families and four predicates do not establish universal behavior.
- This is a preliminary workshop result and a design-space diagnosis, not a production cuVS API.
