#+title: ArXiv-EMIS exact locality and traversal diagnostic

* Verdict

EMIS is heterogeneous but negative for the typical query in exact distance space: filtered neighbors lie substantially deeper than an independent-label model predicts. At the same time, passing nodes are clustered in the CAGRA graph.  This combination explains the result: an ordinary raw-distance frontier has difficulty entering a category region, but once NaviX finds a passing seed its passing-only frontier and two-hop bridges exploit the strong within-category graph connectivity.

The pooled exact top-32 local-selectivity lift is 1.3655 and the top-1024 lift is 1.3773; the per-query top-1024 median is 0.2339, and 0.7460 of queries are below the independence baseline.  The median normalized global rank of the tenth filtered neighbor is 4.9345; 1.0 is the independence expectation.  Thus distance locality is measured directly rather than inferred from graph-search recall.

The pooled and per-query statistics differ because EMIS mixes very different categories. At top-1024, 23/27 categories, representing 6973 queries, have mean lift below 1.  Category 3 alone represents 2010 queries and has 3.1802 lift, which pulls the pooled statistic above 1.

On graph edges, a passing parent has 8.3651x the global chance of reaching another passing node, while a rejected parent has 0.3254x.  The query-weighted largest weak passing component contains 0.9911 of passing nodes.

At matched SINGLE_CTA L=64, W=1, B0, default CAGRA plus the passing accumulator returns 0.5496 recall, versus 0.9681 for strict bitmap-seeded NaviX: a 0.4186 absolute gap with the same graph and filter semantics.  The accumulator fixes output eviction; it cannot fix which graph regions the fused frontier explores.

* Interpretation

- Lift above 1 in exact distance neighborhoods is positive distance/filter correlation; lift below 1 would be negative correlation.
- Passing-parent graph lift above 1 measures category clustering.  Low rejected-parent lift means a raw traversal outside the category has relatively few entrances.
- The GT-stage waterfall separates neighbors checked, admitted, retained, and returned. Here the combination of negative distance locality for most queries, strong passing-node graph clustering, and a large default/NaviX gap points to category entry, frontier lifecycle, and bridge selection—not accumulator loss alone.
